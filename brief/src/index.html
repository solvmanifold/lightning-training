Deployment decision

Do not deploy the adapter.

The LoRA removed 79.3% of prompt tokens, but missed the precommitted quality gate, produced 26 schema-invalid outputs, and was slower through the reliable eager-mode serving path.

The evidence

Prompting earned the baseline.

Before training, a controlled prompt ladder raised exact success from 29.69% to 96.88% on the same 64 development cases. The frozen prompt then held 94.92% on the untouched test.

Experiment design

Built to make “no” credible.

Deterministic generation, disjoint language families, two seeds, development-only selection, and a single opening of the locked test separated training progress from deployment evidence.

NVIDIA recipe alignment

Architecture-aware, not improvised.

The training path preserves NVIDIA NeMo AutoModel’s Nemotron-specific choices: BF16 base weights, single-GPU FSDP2, activation checkpointing, Transformer Engine attention, torch_mm experts, Mamba output-projection exclusion, and repeated MTP loss.

Operational reality

Serving did not rescue the trade.

NIM’s compiled LoRA restart hit one CUDA illegal-instruction failure on GB10. Eager mode reloaded reliably, but was slower. These single-request observations are not a cache-throughput benchmark; they simply provide no compensating latency benefit.

Best next move

Keep the prompt.
Keep the test locked.

Use prompting, constrained output, tooling, RAG, and prefix KV caching first. Fine-tune again only for a real, representative residual gap—with broader surface families and an entirely new locked test.
